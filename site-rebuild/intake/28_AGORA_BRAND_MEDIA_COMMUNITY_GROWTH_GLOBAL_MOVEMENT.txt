DOCUMENT 28 / 39
AGORA BRAND, MEDIA, COMMUNITY GROWTH & GLOBAL MOVEMENT
Version: 1.0 | Strategic working draft | October 2026
Status: Proposed communications strategy. No land ownership, permits, financing, affiliations or operational results are asserted unless independently verified.

01. EXECUTIVE THESIS
Agora must become recognizable because it consistently builds useful things, not because it makes extravagant promises. The communications engine turns a real-world settlement experiment into a transparent, documented, participatory learning movement. Its first task is to attract the right contributors and customers to Agora-01; its second is to make verified lessons transferable across the Agora Network.

02. THE ONE-SENTENCE POSITIONING
Agora is a practical experiment in building healthier, more resilient and more cooperative places to live—starting with a small pilot near Austin and sharing what works.

03. LONG-FORM BRAND PROMISE
We test how people can live well together while producing more of what they need locally: shelter, food, energy, knowledge, culture and opportunity. We combine hands-on construction, community, entrepreneurship, science and responsible technology. We publish progress and failures, welcome constructive disagreement and do not ask anyone to surrender personal autonomy.

04. BRAND BOUNDARIES
Agora is not a sovereign state, a replacement for public institutions, a guaranteed investment, a medical treatment, a religious organization or a promise of immediate self-sufficiency. It is not anti-government, anti-science or anti-business. Do not market a private commercial hospitality operation as a charity. Do not claim nonprofit recognition before confirmation.

05. THE FOUNDING STORY
Eugene and Gennady Fokin are developing a founder-led initiative with an intended 50/50 commercial ownership structure. Their working concept begins with approximately 18 acres near Austin, Texas, potentially including a lake. Land acquisition, legal permissions and financing remain subject to verification. The first pilot targets December 15, 2026 and an initial livable operating base targets January 15, 2027, conditional on closing, safety and approvals. Their distinctive advantage is practical construction and field execution; the story should show that capability rather than invent credentials.

06. MASTER NARRATIVE: FOUR HORIZONS
Horizon I: Agora-01, a testable site with real costs and real constraints.
Horizon II: Agora Network, replicable settlements of different sizes.
Horizon III: Agora OS, tools for operations, learning and transparent decisions.
Horizon IV: Agora Civilization, long-range resilience, research and preservation of knowledge.
Every public presentation should distinguish current operations, funded next steps, aspirational plans and distant speculation.

07. THE CORE MESSAGE SYSTEM
Primary message: Think 100 years. Build in 100 days. Test every week.
Supporting messages: Build before you preach. Freedom requires responsibility. Community is a practice. Measure what matters. Nature and technology can reinforce each other. Useful knowledge should outlive its creators.
Avoid slogans suggesting guaranteed longevity, limitless abundance or complete autonomy.

08. AUDIENCE SEGMENTATION
A. Local guests and retreat customers seeking lawful, well-run experiences.
B. Builders, farmers, engineers and skilled volunteers seeking practical projects.
C. Prospective residents seeking values alignment and clear rights.
D. Researchers, educators and universities seeking an ethical field testbed.
E. Vendors, sponsors and institutional partners seeking defined collaboration.
F. Supporters, donors and impact investors, each requiring distinct legal disclosures.
G. Journalists and creators seeking verifiable stories.
H. Neighbors and local officials who need timely, respectful information.

09. VALUE PROPOSITION BY AUDIENCE
Guests: safe, distinctive experiences with transparent amenities and rules.
Contributors: meaningful work, clear scope, recognition and learning.
Residents: a voluntary community with predictable expectations and due process.
Researchers: consent-based experiments and reproducible documentation.
Partners: concrete deliverables and measurable pilots.
Neighbors: proactive communication, impact mitigation and accessible complaints.
Never promise employment, immigration benefits, land rights or returns through informal membership marketing.

10. VISUAL IDENTITY PRINCIPLES
Visual language: grounded, warm, intelligent, modular and human. Show real hands, materials, site maps, food, landscape, learning and community interaction. Use consistent typography, restrained color palettes and legible mobile-first graphics. Design for both hospitality and technical documentation. Distinguish concept renders with prominent labels; do not pass AI-generated scenes off as completed facilities.

11. BRAND ARCHITECTURE
Agora: umbrella identity and philosophy.
Agora-01: the first specific location and experiment.
Agora Network: replication and operator standards.
Agora OS: digital operations platform.
Agora Research: research activities or proposed institute, subject to entity and naming decisions.
Agora Knowledge Ark: preservation and open educational resources.
Subbrands should not imply separate legal entities where none exist.

12. TONE OF VOICE
Optimistic without hype; technically literate without jargon overload; welcoming without ideological purity tests; ambitious without grandiosity; candid about setbacks. Say “we are testing” when evidence is preliminary and “we measured” only when methods and results can be shown. Use English as the primary public language where appropriate, with Russian and other translations for relevant communities; maintain one canonical factual record.

13. CONTENT PILLARS
1) Build Log: weekly construction and operations.
2) People of Agora: consent-based participant stories.
3) Living Systems: water, energy, food, habitat and ecology.
4) Culture & Gathering: art, meals, shared rituals and voluntary participation.
5) Open Lab: experiments, methods and failures.
6) Future Systems: AI, robotics and resilient settlement design.
7) Economics & Transparency: real budgets and decision tradeoffs.
8) Knowledge Ark: practical educational content.

14. CONTENT EVIDENCE LADDER
Level 0: speculative idea, labeled concept.
Level 1: proposed design, no procurement.
Level 2: prototype or funded plan.
Level 3: operating pilot with dates and evidence.
Level 4: measured results with methodology.
Level 5: independently replicated results.
Attach an evidence level to substantive claims in presentations and reports.

15. CHANNEL STRATEGY
Owned: official website, newsletter, project journal, document library, member onboarding portal.
Social: Instagram for human stories and visual progress; YouTube for documentary and tutorials; LinkedIn for institutional partnerships; optional short-form video for discoverability. Community channels may include a moderated discussion space, but never make a private chat the sole source of official rules.
Earned: local press, podcasts, university talks, partner case studies.
Paid: small, trackable experiments only after the landing page and lawful offering are ready.

16. WEBSITE INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE
Home / Mission / Agora-01 / Build Journal / Participate / Visit (only when permitted) / Research / Partners / FAQ / Contact / Policies. Add a clearly dated project-status panel: parcel status, permitted activities, construction status, safety readiness, booking availability and next milestones. Maintain separate pages for commercial services, charitable initiatives and investment opportunities.

17. FIRST LANDING PAGE: MINIMUM VIABLE RELEASE
One credible hero statement, authentic founder introduction, site concept clearly labeled, 3-5 specific workstreams, a transparent status box, newsletter signup with consent, a contributor interest form, contact email and a plain-language FAQ. No fabricated testimonials, photos of non-existent facilities or countdowns implying certainty.

18. DOCUMENTARY CONTENT FORMAT
Episode structure: question -> baseline -> constraints -> decision -> work -> measurable outcome -> what failed -> next test. Use before/after footage with dates and identical framing when possible. Film a small recurring “Agora Weekly Build Review” with safety and privacy checks before publication.

19. EDITORIAL CADENCE
Weekly: one factual build update, 2-4 short clips or photo stories, one community prompt.
Monthly: one budget-and-milestones report, one long-form interview or technical explainer, one lessons-learned post.
Quarterly: a public scorecard covering costs, infrastructure reliability, participation, incidents in aggregate, and next-quarter priorities. Scale frequency to actual team capacity rather than producing filler.

20. LAUNCH CAMPAIGN: THREE PHASES
Phase A—Pre-site: publish mission, team, design assumptions, parcel due-diligence questions and ways to contribute expertise.
Phase B—Site secured and approved: document baseline conditions, clean-up, infrastructure installation and safety gates.
Phase C—Operating pilot: show verified facilities, authorized activities, visitor experience, feedback and financial learning.
A missed target date triggers a transparent revised plan, not silent deletion of old claims.

21. DECEMBER 15, 2026 FIELD TEST COMMUNICATION
Present as a conditional internal field-test target, not a public opening. If the site, fire safety, sanitation, occupancy and insurance are not ready, hold an off-site planning gathering or postpone. Publish only the verified outcome. A small, safe pilot is a better story than a premature public event.

22. COMMUNITY FUNNEL
Discover -> understand mission -> read FAQ and rules -> express interest -> screening for role-specific competence or suitability -> orientation -> scoped participation -> feedback -> recurring contribution. Track consent, safety training and role boundaries. Do not equate social followers with residents or committed builders.

23. MEMBERSHIP COMMUNICATION
Clearly distinguish free supporters, paying customers, volunteers, members, employees, contractors and residents. State what each receives, pays, owes and can decide. Membership does not automatically confer property equity, voting control, residency, employment or tax-deductible donation status. Formal rights arise only from applicable governing documents and law.

24. RECRUITMENT WITHOUT EXPLOITATION
Publish specific opportunities with tasks, schedule, supervision, risks, accessibility, compensation or volunteer terms and expected learning. Consult employment and labor counsel before using unpaid work in a for-profit operation. Never exchange unsafe labor for vague promises of future ownership or housing.

25. LOCAL COMMUNITY RELATIONS
Create a neighbor information sheet with contact, proposed activities, parking plan, noise controls, fire and wastewater safeguards, and complaint process. Introduce the project to nearby residents before large gatherings. Track and resolve complaints. A good neighbor is more valuable than a viral launch.

26. INCLUSION & CULTURE
Invite people across backgrounds and viewpoints who can respect nonviolence, consent, personal dignity and shared rules. Encourage art, gifting and participation inspired by Burning Man without implying formal affiliation. Avoid shaming individuals for lifestyle differences; define conduct standards rather than ideological tests.

27. EVENTS AS A MEDIA ENGINE
Events can produce content, referrals and revenue, but only when the venue's land use, fire, food service, noise, occupancy, parking, insurance and other requirements are satisfied. Obtain attendee permission for filming; provide no-film zones. Event attendance is not a substitute for operational profitability.

28. MEDIA SAFETY AND PRIVACY
Use written image releases where appropriate, especially for identifiable interviews. Obtain guardian consent for minors and avoid sharing their real-time location or sensitive details. Do not publish precise locations of vulnerable residents, security layouts, access codes or medical information. Respect requests to withdraw future use where legally and operationally possible.

29. AI-GENERATED CONTENT POLICY
AI may assist with editing, translation, diagrams and concept visualization. Label synthetic visuals that could reasonably be mistaken for reality. Never create fake resident testimonials, fabricated press coverage, misleading construction progress or invented partner endorsements. Keep source files and an editorial audit trail.

30. CRISIS COMMUNICATION
Appoint one trained spokesperson and one backup. For emergencies: prioritize response and affected people; verify facts; avoid speculation; coordinate with authorities and legal counsel; provide a dated holding statement; update when material facts change; document corrections. Never publish personal health details or identify complainants without authorization.

31. MISINFORMATION AND REPUTATION RESPONSE
Maintain a public corrections log. Separate legitimate criticism from harassment. Respond with evidence, not personal attacks. Preserve records of material allegations and route safety, fraud, abuse or discrimination complaints through independent channels. Do not suppress truthful criticism to protect the brand.

32. FOUNDER VISIBILITY VS INSTITUTIONAL CONTINUITY
Eugene and Gennady may be primary storytellers at launch, but Agora's identity must not depend on either person's personal social accounts. Use organization-owned domains, shared administrative access, documented passwords and succession procedures. Founder commercial control does not override participant rights or nonprofit independence.

33. PARTNER AND SPONSOR DISCLOSURE
Obtain written permission before using names or logos. Disclose material sponsorships, gifted equipment and affiliate arrangements in content. Separate editorial judgments from sponsor demands. Research results should not be altered to satisfy funders. All investment-related communications require appropriate legal review.

34. FUNDRAISING MESSAGING
Separate three asks: purchase a legitimate service, contribute to an eligible charitable entity, or evaluate a regulated investment offering. Explain use of funds, refund rules, restrictions and risks. Never describe an unregistered entity's receipts as tax-deductible. Do not promise returns or transferable settlement equity without legal review.

35. BRAND INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY
Perform trademark and domain clearance before final brand rollout. Record ownership of logos, footage, photographs, software and written work. Contractors should have clear licenses or assignments. Define permitted use of the Agora name by independent settlement operators. Avoid claiming exclusive rights to common words without counsel.

36. MEDIA OPERATING TEAM
At minimum: editorial owner, field photographer/videographer, fact checker or operations approver, community manager and privacy/safety reviewer; one person may fill multiple roles initially. Every public claim about land, safety, finances, research or partners requires approval by the accountable workstream lead. Maintain an asset library with dates, permissions and captions.

37. LOW-BUDGET TOOLING
Begin with a domain, basic website, email newsletter, shared content calendar, simple CRM, cloud media storage with access controls, analytics and backup. Use smartphones and a modest microphone before purchasing production equipment. Prefer tools with export capability to avoid platform lock-in.

38. BUDGET DISCIPLINE
Within the approximate $25,000 initial site-development assumption, brand spending should remain small and explicitly subordinate to sanitation, safe power, fire protection, insurance and other essential operations. Working hypothesis: $0-$300 for early identity assets, $100-$600 for domain/site/email setup, $0-$500 for basic filming tools if not already owned, and $0-$300/month for ongoing software; validate current pricing before commitments. Do not use these illustrative ranges as vendor quotes.

39. MEASUREMENT FRAMEWORK
North-star metric: qualified contributors, partners and paying customers who advance verified site milestones, not raw impressions.
Leading metrics: newsletter opt-in conversion, qualified applications, site-tour inquiries, partner meetings, content completion, returning participants.
Outcome metrics: volunteer hours legally and safely delivered, documented project milestones, bookings and net contribution margin, retention, neighbor complaints resolved, published experiments replicated.
Trust metrics: corrections issued promptly, consent compliance, factual claim accuracy and complaints closed fairly.

40. 90-DAY EXPERIMENTS
Experiment 1: Two landing-page headlines; compare qualified inquiries rather than clicks.
Experiment 2: Weekly build log; measure retention and useful responses.
Experiment 3: Three contributor calls—builders, researchers, local hosts—with role-specific forms.
Experiment 4: A transparent cost diary; assess trust and partner interest.
Experiment 5: One small lawful gathering or online briefing; track follow-through.
Experiment 6: A bilingual English/Russian explainer; test comprehension, not just reach.
Record hypotheses, sample size, costs, results and decision to continue or stop.

41. FIRST 100 DAYS: EXECUTION ROADMAP
Days 1-15: approve brand thesis, evidence rules, spokesperson, naming inventory and project status sheet.
Days 16-30: launch minimum website, newsletter, CRM and consent process; publish founder story with verified facts.
Days 31-45: create editorial calendar, production templates and initial technical explainers; contact immediate neighbors when parcel is identified.
Days 46-60: start documented build updates if lawful site access exists; otherwise document due diligence and prototypes.
Days 61-75: run contributor recruitment and partner briefing with clear deliverables.
Days 76-90: publish first metrics report and corrections log; revise audience priorities.
Days 91-100: package the repeatable communications playbook for Agora Network.

42. KEY RISKS AND COUNTERMEASURES
Hype outruns execution -> evidence labels and dated status panel.
Founder dependency -> organization-owned channels and documented roles.
Privacy violation -> consent controls and publication review.
Regulatory misrepresentation -> legal/operations fact check.
Cult-like public perception -> voluntary participation, dissent channels and transparent rights.
Unqualified applicants -> role-specific screening and training.
Community conflict -> enforce conduct rules and independent grievance paths.
Paid ads burn cash -> cap tests and require conversion evidence.
Brand dilution across sites -> licensed standards and periodic audits.

43. GO / NO-GO GATES FOR PUBLIC CLAIMS
“Land secured” requires closing or legally adequate control.
“Open for stays” requires lawful occupancy, safety readiness and insurance.
“Research partnership” requires written authorization from the institution.
“Nonprofit” requires an accurate description of legal status.
“Self-sufficient” requires defined metrics and demonstrated performance.
“Agora Network location” requires a signed agreement and standards review.
If the gate fails, use conditional language or omit the claim.

44. OPEN DECISIONS FOR FOUNDERS
Confirm official naming and available domains/handles; decide the public-facing spokesperson; determine whether hospitality and movement accounts are separate; approve initial content budget; choose languages; identify publication and privacy approvers; set brand-use policy for independent chapters; define which metrics can be public without exposing sensitive business information.

45. DELIVERABLES AND HANDOFF
A. One-page positioning brief.
B. Visual identity starter kit.
C. Fact-checked public project status page.
D. Website and newsletter.
E. Editorial calendar and filming consent template.
F. Community onboarding funnel.
G. Partner media kit with evidence labels.
H. Monthly impact-and-operations report.
I. Crisis communication protocol.
J. Agora Network brand replication manual.

46. FINAL PRINCIPLE
A movement becomes credible when its story survives contact with reality. Show the actual site, the actual work, the actual costs, the people who consent to participate and the lessons that others can reproduce. Build trust slowly enough that it can endure rapid growth.
