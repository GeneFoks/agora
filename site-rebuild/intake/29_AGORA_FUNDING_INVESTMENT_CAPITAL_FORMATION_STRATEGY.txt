AGORA FUNDING, INVESTMENT & CAPITAL FORMATION STRATEGY
Version: Working Draft 1.0 | October 2026
Status: Strategic planning, not an offering memorandum, legal opinion, or financial forecast.

MISSION
Finance Agora-01 without losing the ability to operate responsibly, then establish repeatable, transparent capital structures for Agora Network, Agora OS, and a legally distinct public-benefit research/education mission. Capital is a means to build demonstrably useful settlements, not a substitute for viable unit economics.

01. CAPITAL PRINCIPLES
1. Secure the land and legal permission before funding irreversible site improvements.
2. Protect cash runway before discretionary amenities or speculative technology.
3. Never describe investor money as a donation or donation money as investor equity.
4. Separate founder ownership, resident rights, guest contracts, lenders, investors, and nonprofit governance.
5. Disclose all material related-party transactions, conflicts, fees, risks, and ownership interests.
6. Preserve control only through lawful, disclosed governance documents, not hidden arrangements.
7. Finance each milestone with the cheapest appropriate risk-adjusted source of capital.
8. Avoid guaranteed-return language. Publish ranges, assumptions, and downside cases.
9. Prefer evidence of demand, permits, and completed operations over a grand narrative alone.
10. No member should be pressured to invest to belong to the community.

02. FOUNDERS AND OWNERSHIP BASELINE
Working assumption: Eugene and Gennady Fokin are 50/50 founders. Before accepting outside capital, execute a founder agreement covering cash and noncash contributions, management roles, vesting or buy-sell rules if appropriate, deadlock, dilution, IP, guarantees, compensation, and exit. Equity equality does not imply unlimited authority to spend or bind the other founder. Both founders should approve debt, guarantees, real-estate sales, investor issuances, and related-party transactions above documented thresholds.

03. THE THREE CAPITAL LANES
A. LAND / PROPERTY: a property-owning entity with documented title, debt, insurance, permitted uses, and leases.
B. OPERATIONS / TECHNOLOGY: appropriately structured operating entities for hospitality, events, services, and Agora OS/IP, with arm's-length agreements where entities transact.
C. PUBLIC BENEFIT: a separate, compliant nonprofit, if and when justified, with independent fiduciary governance, eligible charitable purposes, restrictions on private benefit, and accurate donor disclosures. Do not presume nonprofit approval or tax deductibility.

Target site: approximately 18 acres near Austin, potentially including a lake.

05. LAND PURCHASE CASH-NEED FORMULA
Closing cash = down payment + closing/title/legal/survey/inspection costs + prepaid taxes/insurance + lender-required reserves + immediate safety work.
Minimum operating liquidity = monthly fixed cash burn x reserve months.
Launch capital required = closing cash + permitted initial improvements + operating liquidity + contingency.
Do not claim the project can launch with $30,000 plus $25,000 until these additional categories have been quoted.

06. ILLUSTRATIVE FINANCING SENSITIVITY
If $220,000 is amortized over 120 months, approximate principal-and-interest payments are:
6% APR: $2,443/month; 8%: $2,669/month; 10%: $2,907/month; 12%: $3,157/month.
These are mathematical examples, not available financing offers; exclude property tax, insurance, maintenance, balloon payments, closing fees, and escrow. A seller-finance deal could have different amortization and a balloon; read the actual note.

07. LAND TRANSACTION GATES
Before nonrefundable commitments: title commitment and exceptions, legal road access, deed restrictions, city limits/ETJ, FEMA flood risk, wetlands and lake regulation, floodplain development, septic feasibility, drinking water, power capacity, fire access, occupancy/use approvals, commercial lodging/event restrictions, and insurability. Verify mineral/pipeline easements, survey boundaries, and environmental concerns. Written authority responses and professional reports outrank verbal assurances.

08. CAPITAL STACK PRIORITY
Tier 1: founders' documented contributions and in-kind labor/materials with provenance and safety checks.
Tier 2: seller financing or suitable secured property debt, with manageable covenants and no concealed personal guarantee exposure.
Tier 3: customer pre-sales only for lawful, realistically deliverable services with transparent refunds.
Tier 4: project-level strategic partners for specific permitted amenities or services.
Tier 5: compliant equity investment when a validated business model and legal offering path exist.
Tier 6: grants and philanthropic support solely for eligible activities and purposes.
Tier 7: expansion financing after Agora-01 has repeatable operating evidence.

09. CONTRIBUTIONS REGISTER
Record every founder contribution: cash, reimbursable expense, loan, donated item, equipment use, and labor. State whether each is equity, debt, reimbursement, or noncompensated effort BEFORE it is contributed. For reused construction materials, obtain owner permission, verify chain of title and suitability, and document condition, code compliance, and installation responsibility.

11. PHASED FUNDING MILESTONES
Phase 0 — Validation: parcel diligence, founder charter, use permissions, preliminary budget, demand interviews. Spend only on high-information tests.
Phase 1 — Acquisition: title, finance documents, insurance, legal access, reserve funding.
Phase 2 — Minimum Viable Settlement: safe permitted sanitation, water, power, shelter, emergency access; founders' occupancy only when legal.
Phase 3 — Revenue Proof: compliant guest stays/events/services; track real contribution margin.
Phase 4 — Optimization: reinvest in capacity, reliability, repeat bookings, and better unit economics.
Phase 5 — Replication: only after at least two reliable operating seasons or a comparably strong evidence threshold.

12. DECEMBER 15, 2026 AND JANUARY 15, 2027 GATES
The proposed December 15 field-test event and January 15 founder occupancy are targets conditional on closing, permitted use, inspections, safe water/sanitation, insurance, and emergency readiness. If any gate fails, host a non-overnight offsite gathering or postpone occupancy. Do not treat a deadline as permission to bypass safety or law.

13. SOURCES OF NON-DILUTIVE CAPITAL
Founder savings; clearly documented founder loans; commercially reasonable supplier credit; lawful refundable bookings; paid workshops with permits; vendor sponsorship with deliverables; research contracts; appropriate grants to eligible recipients. Assess obligations, taxes, refunds, conflicts, and reputational impact. A grant is not 'free cash' and cannot fund unrelated commercial activity.

14. SPONSORSHIP POLICY
Sponsors may supply equipment, materials, software, services, or cash for visible pilots. Every deal needs deliverables, brand-use rules, safety responsibility, warranty terms, exclusivity limits, termination, and honest disclosure. Reject sponsorship that dictates research outcomes or misrepresents products as independently tested.

15. INVESTOR PRODUCT DESIGN
Separate potential investment instruments: property equity, secured notes, operating-company equity, project-specific revenue share, and technology venture equity. Never mix their economics casually. For each, specify issuer, security, use of proceeds, ownership, dilution, voting, distributions, transfer restrictions, default, and exit scenarios. Engage US securities counsel before solicitation; even small private offerings can trigger federal/state securities rules.

16. COMMUNITY CAPITAL AND COOPERATIVE OPTIONS
Possible future models: member purchasing clubs, equipment-sharing pools, housing cooperatives, community investment vehicles, or patronage programs. Each has different tax, housing, securities, lending, consumer, and governance implications. Do not imply membership fees confer real-estate ownership, financial returns, or voting rights unless the signed governing documents expressly do so.

17. FOUNDER CONTROL WITH INVESTOR FAIRNESS
Use clearly disclosed reserved matters, board composition, vetoes for extraordinary actions, and conflict procedures. Minority investors require accurate financial information, lawful rights, and protection against self-dealing. Founders should not promise permanent control regardless of default, financing covenants, or future legal obligations.

18. NONPROFIT INTEGRITY
If an Agora Institute/Foundation is created, its board must pursue the nonprofit's own mission. Transactions with founder-owned companies require conflict-of-interest procedures, fair-market terms, independent approval, and appropriate records. Restricted gifts stay restricted. No private benefit, disguised founder subsidy, or unsupported tax-deduction claims.

19. REVENUE ENGINES TO TEST
A. RV/glamping nights — subject to zoning, health, fire, and short-term rental requirements.
B. Sauna/wellness sessions — subject to safety, accessibility, and permits.
C. Workshops/retreats — instructor fees, insurance, attendance, and food-service rules.
D. Events — noise, parking, sanitation, fire occupancy, and commercial-use approvals.
E. Farm products — agricultural and food-handling rules.
F. Agora OS services — software contracts, cybersecurity, support, and IP licensing.
Do not forecast revenue from a use that has not been cleared for the actual parcel.

20. UNIT ECONOMICS TEMPLATE
For each product: net revenue = gross bookings minus refunds, discounts, and sales taxes collected on behalf of authorities. Contribution margin = net revenue minus payment fees, cleaning, supplies, incremental labor, utilities, maintenance wear, and channel fees. Operating profit then deducts fixed staffing, debt service classification, insurance, property costs, administration, and depreciation as applicable. Cash flow is not accounting profit.

21. THREE-SCENARIO UNDERWRITING
Conservative: 6–12 months of slower-than-planned permitting or demand, higher sitework costs, no event revenue, higher interest and insurance.
Base: only verified legal uses, quoted capital costs, realistic occupancy from comparable markets, explicit seasonality.
Upside: improved occupancy, repeat business, and new services only after evidence.
Show monthly cash flow for 24 months and a five-year annual projection. Include debt coverage, break-even occupancy, downside runway, and required founder cash injections.

22. STRESS TESTS
Model: 25% lower bookings; 30% higher sitework; a 90-day permit delay; major weather closure; one RV replacement; insurance premium increase; founder unable to work for 60 days; no refinance at maturity; software revenue zero. Record which risks cause insolvency and what triggers a spending freeze.

23. CASH MANAGEMENT AND CONTROLS
Separate business bank accounts by entity. Monthly reconciliation, receipt capture, purchase approval limits, dual authorization above a threshold, vendor vetting, inventory register, tax calendar, debt covenant calendar, and cash forecast updated every week during launch. No personal expenses charged to project accounts without legitimate documented treatment.

24. CAPITAL ALLOCATION ORDER
1. Legal compliance and safety.
2. Water, sanitation, access, and insurability.
3. Debt and essential reserves.
4. Revenue-generating assets with proven demand.
5. Reliability and maintenance.
6. Education/research pilots with measurable outcomes.
7. Nonessential amenities, brand spectacle, and speculative R&D.

25. PROJECT FINANCE DASHBOARD
Track cash on hand, 13-week forecast, committed vs uncommitted capex, burn rate, debt service, debt-service coverage ratio, occupancy, average daily rate, gross margin by activity, refund liability, insurance renewal, permit status, receivables, deferred maintenance, and restricted nonprofit funds separately.

26. INVESTOR DATA ROOM
Organize: founder charter, entity formation, cap table, property documents, survey/title, environmental and permit correspondence, business plan, budgets, quotations, operating policies, insurance, historical statements, contracts, IP assignments, technology roadmap, risk register, and monthly reporting templates. Mark unverified claims and forward-looking assumptions clearly.

27. INVESTOR COMMUNICATION STANDARD
Quarterly updates: milestones achieved, cash use, financial results vs budget, safety incidents, legal/permitting developments, risks, next-quarter priorities. Disclose missed targets promptly. Never fabricate endorsements, waiting lists, bookings, permits, or valuations.

28. VALUATION AND DILUTION
Land value, hospitality operating earnings, community goodwill, and software IP are distinct valuation questions. Avoid adding aspirational values together as if liquid assets. Model dilution across at least three funding rounds, option pools, liquidation preferences, and debt conversion terms before accepting investment.

29. CAPITAL PARTNER SCREENING
Evaluate source-of-funds legitimacy, reputation, alignment with resident rights, expected returns, patience, operational demands, veto rights, governance, sanctions/AML issues as applicable, and capacity to fund promised commitments. Money that compromises safety, legality, or mission is not strategic capital.

30. PUBLIC FUNDRAISING AND CROWDFUNDING
Donation, reward, equity, and debt crowdfunding are legally different. Use a compliant platform and qualified counsel where required. Do not publicly solicit securities based on a generic social-media campaign without confirming the applicable exemption and disclosure rules.

31. EXPANSION VEHICLE OPTIONS
Property-by-property SPVs can ring-fence liabilities but add accounting, tax, and administrative cost. A network holding company can coordinate trademarks, standards, and Agora OS licensing. Franchise or licensing models may trigger franchise laws even if called 'partnerships.' Choose structures after proof of Agora-01, not before.

32. AGORA OS FINANCIAL CONTROLS
The OS may forecast budgets, reconcile receipts, flag anomalies, and present approvals. It must not autonomously transfer funds, issue securities, sign loans, change ownership, or approve related-party payments. Require human authorization, role-based permissions, audit logs, backup, and independent reconciliation.

33. 100-DAY CAPITAL ROADMAP
Days 1–15: founders' written contributions, target cap table, land diligence checklist, cash-need model.
Days 16–30: parcel-specific cost quotes, finance term sheet, legal and insurance consultations, 13-week forecast.
Days 31–45: acquisition go/no-go decision; finalize contingencies and funding sources.
Days 46–60: safe/approved minimum viable infrastructure; create bookkeeping and reporting controls.
Days 61–75: demand tests for permitted services; sponsor outreach and pilot contracts.
Days 76–90: review actual margins, safety and customer feedback; revise capital plan.
Days 91–100: publish internal investment-readiness report and next-stage gate decision.
Sequence is conditional; closing and permits may extend beyond 100 days.

35. DECISION GATES FOR RAISING OUTSIDE MONEY
Gate A: title and legal uses substantially verified.
Gate B: first-stage budget based on quotes, not guesses.
Gate C: cash runway survives conservative case.
Gate D: founder control and investor rights documented.
Gate E: applicable offering/solicitation rules reviewed by counsel.
Gate F: credible customer evidence or explicit pre-revenue risk disclosure.
Gate G: reporting and accounting systems operational.
If a gate fails, fund diligence or a smaller pilot instead of marketing the full vision as investment-ready.

36. RED-TEAM QUESTIONS
Can the property support the intended legal uses at all? What if the lake is regulated and unusable? Is septic the dominant cost? What if 10-year seller financing has a 3-year balloon? Are founders personally guaranteeing debt? Is the $25,000 improvement budget materially understated? What revenue exists without guests or events? Who owns donated improvements? What happens to residents if the property is foreclosed? Can the nonprofit truly operate independently? Does an investor expect control incompatible with the founder charter?

37. METRICS AND TARGETS
Targets must be established from the baseline, not invented. Initial leading indicators: percentage of permit questions answered in writing; percentage of capex backed by quotes; minimum months of cash runway; ratio of unrestricted cash to 90-day obligations; weekly forecast variance; count of legally cleared revenue activities; pilot gross margin; safety/maintenance liabilities; investor reporting timeliness.

38. OPEN QUESTIONS TO RESOLVE
Exact parcel/address; zoning jurisdiction; price and closing schedule; interest rate/amortization/balloon; lender security and guarantees; verified access and utilities; actual sanitation cost; taxes/insurance; founder cash commitments; desired ownership entities; investment appetite and investor residency; securities counsel; expected hospitality pricing; available labor hours; resident tenancy implications; initial nonprofit purpose and board independence.

39. LONG-HORIZON CAPITAL MODEL
Agora-01 demonstrates a real operating model. Agora Network raises property- and market-specific capital with standardized diligence and reporting. Agora OS can become a separate software business if it solves paid problems beyond the community. Research activities seek grants and contracts only where eligible. At Agora-500/5000 scale, institutional finance may become possible only after audited performance, resilient governance, and replicable unit economics.

40. NON-NEGOTIABLE ETHICAL RULE
Never sell the dream of civilization-scale growth as a promise of financial return. Distinguish community belonging, philanthropic support, real-estate ownership, and securities investment. Consent, transparency, regulatory compliance, and resident welfare are core infrastructure.

41. FINAL CAPITAL THESIS
Think 100 years. Build in 100 days. Test every week. The right financing strategy is not the largest fundraising round: it is the smallest sequence of well-governed investments that makes Agora-01 safe, legal, solvent, useful, and worthy of replication.
