AGORA CIVILIZATION BLUEPRINT 1.0
DOCUMENT 17 — AGORA-01 COMMUNITY, MEMBERSHIP & RESIDENT EXPERIENCE
Version: 0.1 | 9 October 2026 | Status: working draft, not adopted policy

PURPOSE
Build a humane, welcoming, financially sustainable community on the first approximately 18-acre site near Austin, while testing a repeatable operating model for future Agora settlements. Community is not a marketing label: it is the combination of people, reciprocal responsibilities, meaningful shared experiences, rights, conflict resolution, and voluntary participation.

1. FOUNDING CONTEXT
Founders: Eugene and Gennady Fokin; proposed 50/50 founding ownership. Land purchase is not yet confirmed. Working assumptions: $250,000 acquisition, $30,000 down payment, ten-year financing, and $25,000 development spending in the first twelve months, plus founders' work and lawful, safe reuse of materials. Proposed pilot gathering: 15 December 2026; minimum viable settlement target: 15 January 2027. All occupancy, lodging, event, sanitation, and fire-related uses depend on permits, zoning, insurance, and site suitability.

2. COMMUNITY PROMISE
Agora is a place to participate, learn, create, care for the land, and become more capable together. Its culture draws inspiration from Burning Man: gifting, radical inclusion, participation, civic responsibility, communal effort, self-reliance, and leave-no-trace practices. It does not demand conformity or perfection. Physical, cognitive, and emotional growth are invitations, not tests of human worth.

3. DISTINCT USER GROUPS
A. Founders: owners and operational stewards; responsibilities and authority must be explicit.
B. Resident contributors: people living on-site under written occupancy arrangements and clear expectations.
C. Short-stay guests: paid RV/glamping/wellness customers with hospitality protections and no implied membership rights.
D. Community members: opt-in participants who may attend gatherings and contribute projects, with documented benefits and limits.
E. Volunteers: only where lawful, insured, and properly classified; never substitute unpaid labor for employees improperly.
F. Experts and collaborators: educators, engineers, agronomists, artists, researchers, and health professionals.
G. Neighbors: important stakeholders, not obstacles to bypass.

4. MEMBERSHIP MODEL — HYPOTHESES TO TEST
Visitor / Guest: pay for an approved event or stay; no governance rights.
Community Member: free or low-cost opt-in membership for communication, invitations, and volunteer opportunities.
Supporting Member: optional recurring dues in exchange for precisely stated benefits; illustrative test only, not a promised subscription.
Resident Member: separate occupancy agreement plus community participation terms; residency does not confer equity.
Project Partner: written project-specific contract and, if applicable, separately negotiated economics.
Avoid selling unlimited access or benefits that the pilot cannot deliver. Do not promise membership across future locations until those locations exist.

5. THE RESIDENT JOURNEY
Discover -> attend an open event -> review written code and expectations -> short trial visit -> mutual-fit conversation -> verify eligibility and occupancy conditions -> sign relevant agreement -> orientation -> participate -> periodic feedback -> voluntary exit or documented offboarding.
Never require disclosure of religion, political beliefs, medical history, or intimate personal information as a condition of ordinary community belonging. Apply housing and public-accommodation laws, including nondiscrimination requirements.

6. COMMUNITY RITUALS
Weekly shared meal; voluntary workday; skills exchange; fireside conversation where fires are permitted; monthly community forum; seasonal land stewardship day; collaborative art; wellness activities; reflection and learning sessions. Offer alcohol-free default gatherings without stigmatizing individuals. Rules for smoking, intoxication, and prohibited conduct should be explicit, lawful, proportionate, and uniformly applied.

7. PERSONAL DEVELOPMENT WITHOUT COERCION
Provide opportunities for movement, nutritious food, reading, making, creativity, and practical education. No compulsory spirituality, ideological tests, forced exercise, mandatory sharing, or public ranking of residents. Consent and privacy matter. People may contribute in different ways and at different levels of ability.

8. GOVERNANCE AND TRUST
Founders retain clearly disclosed ownership and reserved corporate powers. Community can have real input on programming, shared-space norms, stewardship priorities, and feedback. Define which decisions are consultative versus binding. Publish understandable policies, meeting notes where appropriate, and a conflict-of-interest procedure. Establish a founder deadlock process in separate governing documents.

9. COMMUNITY CODE — DRAFT
Respect boundaries and consent. No harassment, threats, discrimination, or violence. Respect property, quiet hours, shared resources, animals, and the lake. Follow fire, sanitation, and food-safety rules. Do not use community membership to pressure others into labor, money transfers, romantic relationships, or belief systems. Report safety concerns promptly. Rules must include a fair warning, appeal, and emergency-removal process as appropriate.

10. SUBSTANCES AND SAFETY
Do not facilitate or conceal unlawful drug activity. Any discussions of altered-state practices must remain separate from Agora-sponsored programming unless independently lawful, professionally reviewed, insured, and compliant with all applicable rules. The site should have clear policies on impairment, emergency response, and safe conduct. Avoid using unverified medical or wellness claims in promotion.

11. RESIDENT OPERATIONS
Document occupancy eligibility, length of stay, payment terms, utilities, mail, parking, sanitation, trash, quiet hours, guests, pets, maintenance, incident reporting, emergency evacuation, and termination. Verify whether RV and tent residency is lawful before offering it. Use written agreements reviewed for Texas law. Ensure access to drinking water, sanitation, weather shelter, and emergency services.

12. HOSPITALITY VS COMMUNITY
Separate guest booking and payment from membership and residential agreements. Design privacy boundaries: guest-access areas, resident-only areas, equipment/storage, and workspaces. Paid guests are not automatically community members. Community members are not automatically entitled to free accommodation.

13. ECONOMIC COOPERATION
Encourage voluntary internal purchasing, skills exchange, repair, and shared tools. Track contributions transparently. Do not create informal labor obligations, opaque profit promises, or noncompliant nonprofit transfers. Commercial revenue, member dues, gifts, and charitable donations require appropriate separate accounting and disclosures.

14. NEIGHBOR RELATIONS
Before major events, learn the nearby residents' concerns about noise, traffic, lighting, dust, wastewater, fire, and lake impacts. Establish a contact number, event hours, parking plan, and complaint response. Treat neighbor trust as a measurable asset.

15. DECEMBER 15 FIELD TEST
Only if the property is acquired and use is authorized: invite a modest, known group; test arrival, parking, sanitation, lighting, sleeping comfort, food service, sauna rental if lawful, noise, first aid, and cleanup. Assign a safety lead and incident log. Ask attendees what made them feel included, safe, and useful. Do not market this as a public commercial event before approvals.

16. JANUARY 15 MINIMUM VIABLE COMMUNITY
Target: founders can occupy the property legally and safely, maintain basic utilities, and host a small authorized gathering. One functioning sauna is a construction goal, not permission to operate. Verify fire clearances, ventilation, electrical systems, emergency access, and insurance before use.

17. 90-DAY COMMUNITY EXPERIMENTS
Experiment 1: one weekly shared meal; measure return attendance and perceived belonging.
Experiment 2: one volunteer stewardship day; measure task completion and satisfaction.
Experiment 3: one skills exchange; measure useful skills transferred.
Experiment 4: one structured feedback forum; measure resolution of issues.
Experiment 5: guest/resident separation test; measure privacy complaints.
Experiment 6: trial supporting membership; test willingness to pay only after benefits are real and clearly stated.

18. METRICS
Return participation rate; volunteer hours (recorded without coercion); attendee safety incidents; resident satisfaction; complaint response time; neighbor complaints; recurring revenue per member; actual cost to serve; number of shared projects completed; member retention; inclusion and accessibility feedback. Collect only necessary data with clear consent and retention policies.

19. RED TEAM — FAILURE MODES
Founder control perceived as hidden: publish rights transparently.
Community becomes unpaid workforce: classify labor properly and pay where required.
Guests overwhelm residents: separate zones and calendars.
Personality cult or ideological pressure: distribute responsibilities and protect dissent.
Unmanaged interpersonal conflict: fair process and external mediation options.
Unlawful occupancy or events: verify before launch.
Burnout: cap founder commitments and recruit accountable operators.
Revenue promises outrun infrastructure: pilot only what can be safely delivered.

20. LONG-TERM SCALING
Agora-25: simple written charter, weekly forums, part-time community steward.
Agora-100: dedicated resident services, documented dispute process, program calendar.
Agora-500: professional governance and hospitality separation, independent safety functions.
Agora Network: portable membership identity with user consent; site-specific local rights; no automatic transfer of residency or property rights.
Agora OS: opt-in scheduling, booking, incident management, and resource dashboards; no surveillance scoring or automated expulsion decisions.

21. FIRST TEN ACTIONS
1) Confirm parcel jurisdiction and lawful intended uses.
2) Draft a one-page community promise.
3) Draft a plain-language member rights matrix.
4) Create guest, resident, and volunteer agreement templates for legal review.
5) Define smoking, substance, consent, quiet-hour, and fire policies.
6) Identify two emergency contacts and a safety lead.
7) Design one low-cost gathering for the first authorized date.
8) Invite 10–15 prospective founding participants to a listening session.
9) Create a feedback form and simple incident register.
10) Schedule a founder decision meeting on membership scope and pricing.

22. OPEN DECISIONS FOR EUGENE AND GENNADY
What community benefits will be free? Who may reside and for how long? What degree of member voting is appropriate? What is the maximum event size? What are acceptable quiet hours? Are pets allowed? What volunteer activities are permissible? What specific benefits justify paid membership? Who responds to complaints? What privacy standards will Agora OS follow?

CORE IDEA
The first Agora must prove more than the ability to rent beds. It must prove that people freely choose to return, contribute, grow, and trust one another — without sacrificing legal compliance, individual rights, or economic reality.
