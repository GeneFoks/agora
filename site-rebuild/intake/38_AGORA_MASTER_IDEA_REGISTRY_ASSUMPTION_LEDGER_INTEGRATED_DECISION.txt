PURPOSE
Turn the preceding blueprint into a controlled, testable decision system. This is an operating register, not a declaration that any parcel, permit, funding source, legal structure, technical specification, or forecast has been verified. The source documents remain authoritative for detailed concepts; this document establishes cross-document priorities, owners, gates, evidence requirements, and conflict-resolution rules.

01. OPERATING THESIS
Think 100 years. Build in 100 days. Test every week. Agora-01 is a real-world testbed near Austin, Texas, not a claim that a fully autonomous civilization can be delivered in its first season. The minimum useful outcome is a lawful, safe, financially survivable site with repeatable learning and a positive guest/resident experience.

02. EVIDENCE LABELS
CONFIRMED = primary documentation obtained and independently checked where appropriate.
PROVISIONAL = credible indication, pending verification.
ASSUMPTION = planning input with no adequate proof.
HYPOTHESIS = claim requiring an experiment.
BLOCKED = cannot proceed until a specified dependency is resolved.
REJECTED = disproven, unsafe, unlawful, or economically unacceptable.
Every critical statement in the operating dashboard must carry one label, date, source, owner, and next review date.

03. DECISION CLASSES
Class A / irreversible: land purchase, debt, entity ownership, long leases, major excavation, equity grants, construction without easy reversal. Both founders approve in writing after relevant professional review.
Class B / significant: commitments above a founder-agreed threshold, changes to opening scope, public launch claims, new revenue lines, hiring. Approval per delegated authority matrix.
Class C / reversible: small experiments, content drafts, prototype software, low-cost supplies. Single accountable owner within an approved budget.
Class D / emergency: immediate protection of life and property by the on-scene responsible person, with prompt documentation and founder notification.

04. FOUNDERS' DECISION RIGHTS
Proposed equal economics do not automatically define day-to-day authority. Execute an operating agreement defining contributions, vesting if any, capital calls, distributions, ownership of improvements and IP, spending limits, tie-break process, exits, death/disability, disputes, and fiduciary duties. No unilateral transfer of land, ownership, or core IP. Community governance rights must be described truthfully and cannot be withdrawn arbitrarily once contractually granted.

14. THE FIVE NON-NEGOTIABLE LAUNCH GATES
G1 LAND: clear title/rights, acceptable hazards, legal access, workable site plan.
G2 LEGAL: written answers on land use, permits, temporary occupancy, business operations and insurance.
G3 LIFE SAFETY: potable water, approved sanitation, safe electrical, fire response, severe weather and emergency access.
G4 FINANCE: cash to close, reserves, realistic debt service and stop-loss trigger.
G5 GOVERNANCE: signed founder agreement, spending authority, recordkeeping, guest rules and incident ownership.
Failure of any gate means no public opening and no unsafe occupation. A celebration or site visit can be rescheduled or relocated.

19. CONFLICTS ACROSS BLUEPRINT DOCUMENTS
C-001 Founder control versus resident participation: distinguish asset/entity ownership, contractual membership rights, operational consultation and legally required due process.
C-002 Autonomy versus grid dependence: measure actual self-sufficiency; do not call a grid-connected camp fully off-grid.
C-003 Low-budget speed versus safety/permitting: life safety and law take priority over dates and aesthetic scope.
C-004 Hospitality income versus residential community privacy: separate zones, schedules, consent and data.
C-005 Nonprofit mission versus commercial returns: independent governance, arms-length transactions and appropriate restrictions.
C-006 Openness/media versus privacy: opt-in photography and no publication of sensitive resident information.
C-007 Ambitious R&D versus near-term cash flow: cap research spend until essential infrastructure is funded.
C-008 Freedom of expression versus anti-harassment: viewpoint diversity is welcome; threats, abuse and discrimination are not.

20. PRIORITIZATION METHOD
Score each initiative 0–5 for: life-safety/legal necessity (weight 5), dependency unlock (4), economic survival (4), learning value (3), member benefit (2), reversibility (2), implementation effort (negative 2), cash intensity (negative 3), compliance uncertainty (negative 4). Use a documented rubric and sensitivity check. Mandatory legal and safety gates override the numerical score.

22. CASH CONTROL
Maintain separate acquisition, capital improvement, operations and restricted nonprofit accounts where applicable. Weekly cash forecast: opening cash + committed inflows - fixed outflows - contingency - debt obligations. No speculative revenue counts as committed. Define hard stop if projected cash falls below agreed minimum reserve. All reimbursements need receipts and approval.

23. FINANCIAL SCENARIOS
BASE: modest utilization, slow approvals, conservative guest revenue.
DOWNSIDE: no public revenue for six months, cost overrun, financing delays, emergency repair.
UPSIDE: repeatable compliant paid experiences with documented positive margins.
For each scenario calculate monthly debt service, insurance, taxes, utilities, sanitation, maintenance, payroll, marketing, and replacement reserves. No numeric return is credible until parcel-specific inputs exist.

27. MEASUREMENT FRAMEWORK
Leading indicators: verified diligence items, permits secured, cash runway, incident-response readiness, tasks completed on time, hours of founder workload.
Lagging indicators: occupancy (only when lawful), net operating cash flow, incident rates, water and energy per guest-night, resident/guest satisfaction, retention, verified learning outcomes.
Every metric needs a numerator, denominator, collection method, frequency and limitations statement.

29. STOP / PIVOT / PROCEED RULES
STOP: illegal use, material life-safety deficiency, unclear title, insolvency, inability to insure required activities, or founder agreement unresolved before major commitment.
PIVOT: delayed approvals, unaffordable utility extension, weak demand, inadequate volunteer capacity; shrink scope or select a different parcel.
PROCEED: written gate evidence, affordable downside case, trained operators, funded contingencies and accountable owners.
